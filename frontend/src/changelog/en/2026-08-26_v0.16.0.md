## The Shift Says What It Is

Opening a shift used to greet you with the words **Shift Details** in bold — a
label, telling you what you already knew, on the screen you had just chosen to
open. The thing you were actually looking for was underneath: the job's name in
small grey text, and the day and time as the third and fourth of five identical
rows.

Now the job's name is the heading, and the day and time are the line under it.

![A shift, opened on a phone](./images/shift-sheet-mobile.png)

Where and what have moved onto one line instead of two paragraph-sized blocks,
and the number of places is stated once, next to the name, instead of twice in
two different phrasings. On a computer the whole panel is about a third shorter
than it was.

![The same shift, on a computer](./images/shift-dialog-desktop.png)

## Who Is On It — And Who Is Not

The list of people underneath used to be a small spreadsheet: a bordered table
with column headings, inside a panel that already had a border. It listed who
had signed up and stopped there, so the places still waiting for somebody were
something you worked out by subtracting.

It is a list of people now, with faces, and it ends by saying what is left —
**1 spot still free** — rather than leaving you to do the arithmetic.

## Panels Come Up From The Bottom

On a phone, every panel in WirkSam used to open as a small box floating in the
middle of the screen: too narrow to read comfortably, scrolling inside itself
while the page behind it sat still, and with its buttons stranded halfway up
the screen where your thumb is not.

They now rise from the bottom edge, full width, with the button you came for
across the bottom where you can reach it. You can pull one down to dismiss it,
or press the cross.

![Confirming something, on a phone](./images/confirm-sheet-mobile.png)

This is every panel, not just the shift one — confirming a deletion, adding a
shift, cropping a profile picture, starting a demo. Questions that need a yes
or a no put both answers across the full width, one above the other, so there
is no small target to miss.

On a computer nothing about this changes. Panels stay centred, the way they
were.
