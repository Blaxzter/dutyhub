## A Tour That Makes an Argument

"Try a demo event" drops you into a festival that already has four jobs, a
schedule and a handful of volunteers, and a guided tour starts on its own. That
tour used to read every label on the screen to you. It now explains one idea at
a time, in four short chapters, and the screens are the evidence rather than the
subject.

The idea it is explaining is the one thing worth knowing about WirkSam: a job is
something that has to be covered over a stretch of time, a job is cut into
shifts, and a shift is what a person actually takes. Once that lands, the rest
of the app reads itself. The volunteer's tour is ten steps and the organiser's
is eleven — both shorter than what they replace, and both ending on what you can
press next.

## Buttons That Say What They Do

The button that moves the tour along used to say "Next" whether it was moving to
the following sentence or opening a dialog, jumping to another page, or
unfolding part of a form. Now it says which: *Open this shift*, *Create a job*,
*Show me the preview*, *See my shifts*. Nothing happens to the page that the
button did not warn you about first, and the tour scrolls to where it is going
and waits for the page to settle before it points at anything.

## Taking a Shift Actually Works

The step that shows you how to take a shift is the point of the whole demo, and
it was broken. It opened whichever shift happened to be first on the board,
which was reliably one that was already full — so most visitors were shown a
booking button that was not there, and the rest got an error if they pressed it.
The demo now keeps a shift free for you, the tour opens that one, and pressing
the button books it.

Cancelling works too. Demo visitors could book a shift and then find no way to
hand it back, because the app was recognising "your" bookings by email address
and demo accounts do not have one. It recognises you properly now.

If you try the volunteer demo and then the organiser demo without closing the
tab, the second one gets its tour as well. Previously it started in silence.

## Aufgaben, Not Veranstaltungen

In German, tasks were called *Veranstaltungen* — the same word the app uses for
the event that contains them. The sidebar, the create form and the breadcrumbs
all said it, so a tour explaining that an event contains tasks was pointing at
two different things wearing the same name. Tasks are *Aufgaben* now, and
*Veranstaltung* means the event and nothing else. The bottom bar on a phone had
also never been translated at all.
